## Writing Style Patterns (from both examples)

### Headline Patterns
- Application-focused: "High-Precision Laser Ranging Modules for Liquid Level Sensing: An Engineer's Guide to 1D ToF"
- Educational/definitional: "What Is a ToF Sensor and How Does It Work?"

### Content Structure Patterns
1. **Open with a hook** — Problem statement (Example 1) or relatable question (Example 2). Get the reader to nod before diving into specs.
2. **Progressive disclosure** — Simple explanation first, then layer in technical detail. Don't front-load jargon.
3. **Comparison tables** — A well-structured comparison table converts better than paragraphs of prose. Engineers scan, they don't read linearly.
4. **FAQ closes the article** — Natural-language Q&A targets both Google's PAA and AI search prompts. Address objections before the reader has to search elsewhere.
5. **Strong CTA** — Educational content uses soft CTAs; application guides use direct conversion CTAs.

### Tone & Voice
- **Technically authoritative without being academic** — Correct terminology (VCSEL, SPAD, TDC, dToF, iToF) used accurately, but concepts explained from first principles
- **Confident, not arrogant** — Claims backed by specific numbers; competitor alternatives acknowledged without disparagement
- **Benefits tied to specs** — "Works in direct sunlight" not just "100k lux ambient immunity"; "millimeter-level accuracy" not just "±2mm"
- **Conversational openings, technical bodies** — Hook like a human, deliver like an engineer
- **Global engineering audience** — No region-specific idioms, no pop culture references

### Formatting Patterns
- **Short paragraphs** — 2-3 sentences max for scanning
- **Spec tables** — Key specifications presented in scannable table format, not buried in prose
- **Bullet lists for applications** — Each use case gets its own bullet with a concise description
- **Bold for key numbers** — Differentiating specs and benchmarks should pop visually

### FAQ Best Practices
- 3 questions per article is the sweet spot
- Questions mirror how engineers actually ask: "Can 1D ToF see through foam?" not "What are the optical properties of..."
- Answers are direct and honest — acknowledge limitations, then provide the workaround
- Format: bold question, paragraph answer

## Key Takeaways for New Content

1. **Lead with the spec or question that matters** — Engineers evaluate sensors by datasheets. Give them a reason to keep reading in the first 100 words.
2. **Use comparison tables** — Side-by-side evaluation is how engineers make decisions. Make it easy.
3. **Be honest about limitations** — Every sensor has trade-offs. Acknowledging them builds credibility (see foam discussion in Example 1).
4. **Connect specs to applications** — Abstract numbers become concrete value when tied to a real use case.
5. **Close with natural-language FAQs** — This is the single most efficient tactic for capturing both traditional and AI-search traffic.
6. **Keep paragraphs short** — 2-3 sentences max. Engineer readers scan, they don't read linearly.
7. **Use correct technical terminology** — dToF vs iToF, SPAD, VCSEL, TDC, MIPI, I2C. Accuracy is credibility.
