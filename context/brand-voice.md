# DOMI Sensor Brand Voice & Messaging

This document defines the DOMI Sensor brand voice, tone, and messaging framework. Reference this when writing all content to ensure consistency.

## Brand Voice Pillars

### 1. Technically Authoritative
- **What it means**: We are a semiconductor company founded by engineers with 15+ years of tier-1 experience at Analog Devices and STMicroelectronics. Our expertise is real and deep.
- **How it sounds**: Confident, precise, data-driven. Every claim is backed by specs—range in meters, accuracy in millimeters, lux tolerance in real numbers. We don't do vague.
- **Example**: "The DM0301 delivers ranging up to 5 meters with <20 mm accuracy, even under 100,000 lux ambient light—thanks to its integrated VCSEL + SPAD + TDC + MCU architecture."
- **Avoid**: Marketing fluff without specs, exaggerated claims ("revolutionary," "game-changing"), hand-waving technical details.

### 2. Precision as Identity
- **What it means**: Millimeter-level accuracy is our DNA. We're not "good enough"—we're exact. Every sensor, every module, every measurement reflects this obsession.
- **How it sounds**: Specific, numbers-forward, engineering-minded. When we say "high precision," we tell you exactly what that means.
- **Example**: "Sub-centimeter depth accuracy across 0.2–8 meters, with multi-frequency calibration that maintains stability from indoor darkness to direct sunlight."
- **Avoid**: Vague descriptors ("high quality," "excellent performance") without quantification.

### 3. Integration-First, Engineer-Empowering
- **What it means**: We don't just sell components—we help engineers integrate 3D vision into their products. From SDKs to custom optical design, our job is to make their job easier.
- **How it sounds**: Practical, helpful, solution-oriented. We anticipate integration pain points and address them proactively.
- **Example**: "Same footprint. Same pin layout. Same LGA package. Drop the DM0301 into your existing design—no PCB redesign, no firmware rewrite, just longer range and lower cost."
- **Avoid**: Gatekeeping technical knowledge, hiding integration complexity, oversimplifying to the point of inaccuracy.

### 4. Globally Accessible, Deeply Local
- **What it means**: Based in Shenzhen, serving innovators worldwide. We understand the global hardware supply chain and support customers regardless of language or location.
- **How it sounds**: Professional English with clear, direct communication. We respect our readers' intelligence and time. No unnecessary jargon, no cultural assumptions.
- **Example**: "Whether you're prototyping in Silicon Valley or scaling production in Stuttgart, our engineering team responds within 24 hours with the technical support you need."
- **Avoid**: Idioms that don't translate, region-specific references, overly casual language that may confuse international readers.

### 5. Forward-Looking, Vision-Driven
- **What it means**: We're building the "eyes" of future machines—robots, drones, autonomous systems, smart spaces. Our technology enables the next generation of intelligent perception.
- **How it sounds**: Aspirational but grounded. We connect today's technical specs to tomorrow's applications. The future is exciting, but we're realistic about what it takes to get there.
- **Example**: "As autonomous mobile robots move from controlled warehouses to dynamic public spaces, they need depth sensors that work reliably in every lighting condition. That's the future we're engineering today."
- **Avoid**: Science fiction without substance, hype about AI/robotics without technical grounding, buzzwords.

## Tone Guidelines

### General Tone: Senior Engineer Explaining to a Peer
Imagine you're a senior hardware engineer explaining a technical topic to another experienced engineer. You know your stuff cold. You're direct, clear, and respectful of their intelligence. You don't oversimplify, but you also don't bury the point in unnecessary complexity. You lead with what matters and back it with data.

### Tone Variations by Content Type

**Technical Product Pages**: Precise, specification-driven, comparative
- "The DMAS2M001 delivers 40×30 resolution dToF depth maps at 10 FPS across a 60°×45° field of view."
- "Unlike indirect ToF sensors, direct ToF avoids multi-path interference in outdoor environments."
- "Key differentiator: 8-meter maximum range in a 4.4×2.4×1.0mm form factor."

**How-To / Integration Guides**: Practical, step-by-step, encouraging
- "Start by verifying your I2C connection with this simple register read."
- "If you're migrating from a VL53L4CD, the pinout is identical—here's what changes in firmware."
- "One common pitfall: ensure your cover glass transmission exceeds 90% at 940nm."

**Application / Solution Content**: Problem-solution, benefit-focused, use-case-driven
- "Warehouse AMRs need to detect glass walls, dropped pallets, and thin obstacles at speed."
- "In face recognition systems, depth maps prevent spoofing attacks that 2D cameras miss."
- "Privacy regulations restrict RGB cameras in public spaces—ToF people counting solves this with depth-only data."

**Industry Insights / Thought Leadership**: Analytical, forward-looking, informed
- "The shift from iToF to dToF in consumer electronics mirrors what happened in automotive LiDAR."
- "As edge AI matures, 3D depth sensors transition from optional to essential in IoT devices."
- "Three trends driving ToF adoption in 2026: smaller form factors, higher ambient light immunity, and pin-compatible drop-in replacements."

**News / Product Announcements**: Direct, factual, enthusiastic but professional
- "The DMP300KP delivers 300W peak power in a compact 5.2×5.2×1.55mm package on AlN ceramic substrate."
- "Available for sampling now with custom FOV and wavelength binning options."
- "This launch extends DOMI's VCSEL portfolio to address high-power industrial and automotive applications."

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary length**: Mix short declarative sentences ("The DM0301 is a direct replacement.") with longer explanatory ones.
- **Active voice preferred**: "The sensor measures depth" not "Depth is measured by the sensor."
- **Average length**: 15–22 words per sentence.
- **Clarity first**: If a technical concept needs a longer sentence, use it. Clarity beats brevity.

### Paragraph Structure
- **Length**: 2–4 sentences typically.
- **One idea per paragraph**: Spec, benefit, or use case—pick one.
- **Transitions**: Logical, not decorative. Let the technical narrative flow naturally.
- **White space**: Break up specification blocks and comparison tables clearly.

### Word Choice
- **Technical, not academic**: "Measures distance using time-of-flight" not "Utilizes time-of-flight methodology for distance quantification."
- **Specific, never vague**: "Up to 5m range at 100k lux" not "Excellent range in bright conditions."
- **Active and direct**: "Integrates," "delivers," "enables," "supports"—not "provides," "offers," "facilitates."
- **Avoid**: "Very," "really," "extremely," "best-in-class," "industry-leading" (prove it with numbers instead).

### Terminology

**Say This** → **Not That**
- 3D depth sensing → 3D imaging (depth is more precise for our technology)
- Time-of-Flight / ToF → TOF (always capitalize correctly)
- millimeter-level accuracy → very accurate (quantify whenever possible)
- depth map → 3D picture (technical audience expects correct terminology)
- ranging sensor → distance sensor (ranging is the industry-standard term)
- ambient light immunity → works in bright light (use proper engineering terminology)

## Content Formatting

### Headlines
- **Be specific**: "DM0301 1D ToF Sensor: Pin-to-Pin VL53L4CD Replacement with 5m Range" not "A Better ToF Sensor"
- **Include the key spec or benefit**: What makes this worth reading?
- **Numbers and comparisons work**: "3.8× Longer Range," "Half the Cost," "100k Lux"
- **Keep under 70 characters when possible** for SEO.

### Subheadings
- **Descriptive and self-contained**: Should make sense when scanning.
- **Keyword-rich naturally**: Include target keywords where they fit.
- **Use technical terms your audience searches for**: "dToF vs iToF," "SPAD array," "VCSEL emitter."
- **Consistent parallel structure within same-tier sections.**

### Lists
- **Bullets for specs, features, and comparisons**: Engineers scan, they don't read.
- **Numbers for steps, rankings, and priorities.**
- **Parallel structure**: If one bullet starts with a noun, they all do.
- **Tables for head-to-head comparisons**: VL53L4CD vs DM0301 belongs in a table, not paragraphs.

### Calls-to-Action
- **Action-oriented**: "Request a Quote," "Download the Datasheet," "Talk to an Engineer."
- **Specific**: "Get the DM0301 Evaluation Kit" not "Learn More."
- **Match the funnel stage**: Technical articles → "View Full Specs." Comparison articles → "Request a Sample."
- **Place naturally**: After you've made the case, not before.

## Audience Understanding

### Who We Write For

**Primary Audience**: Hardware & Embedded Systems Engineers
- Designing products that need depth sensing: robots, drones, access control, IoT devices
- Evaluate components based on datasheets, specs, and reliability data
- Value pin-compatibility, SDK quality, and supplier responsiveness
- Technical depth earns their trust; marketing fluff loses it
- Located globally, primarily China, North America, Europe

**Secondary Audiences**:
- **Engineering Managers / CTOs**: Evaluating make-vs-buy decisions, supplier qualification, BOM cost
- **Product Designers / System Architects**: Scoping sensor requirements for new products
- **Procurement / Supply Chain**: Sourcing components at scale, second-source qualification
- **Students & Researchers**: Learning about ToF technology, building prototypes

### What They Care About

**Top Priorities**:
1. Spec accuracy and real-world performance (not just datasheet numbers)
2. Integration effort (pin compatibility, SDK support, documentation quality)
3. Reliability and consistency across production volumes
4. Cost competitiveness (unit price + integration cost)
5. Supplier responsiveness and technical support

**Pain Points**:
1. Sensors that don't perform to spec in real conditions (lighting, temperature, target surface)
2. Long lead times for custom modifications or technical support
3. Incomplete or poorly translated documentation
4. Single-source dependency on large semiconductor vendors
5. Balancing performance requirements with BOM cost constraints

### How to Serve Them

- **Lead with specs, follow with context**: Give them the numbers first, then explain why they matter.
- **Be honest about limitations**: If a sensor has a 20mm minimum range, say so. Engineers appreciate transparency more than marketing spin.
- **Show your work**: Reference calibration methods, testing conditions, and measurement standards.
- **Provide comparison data**: Your sensor vs. the industry standard part—same conditions, same metrics.
- **Respect their time**: Technical audiences want to evaluate and decide. Make it easy.
